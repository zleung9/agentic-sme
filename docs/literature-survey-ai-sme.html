Executive Summary: The AI-on-SME Landscape

Key finding

The intersection of AI/ML and single-molecule electronics is surprisingly sparse for 2024–2026. After systematic searches across multiple query strategies, only one publication directly applies deep learning to molecular junction quantum transport simulation (DeePTB-NEGF, Zou et al., 2024). No publications were found that apply generative AI, graph neural networks, or agentic systems to molecular discovery for single-molecule electronics in this period.

Implication for SME-Agent

This near-absence of AI-on-SME work means SME-Agent occupies a nearly empty niche. The key competitive landscape is not other AI systems, but rather:

  1. Expert-rule-based molecular design (Lambert group MRR, Sangtarash heteroatom rules) — heuristic but well-established
  2. Conventional DFT+NEGF screening — computationally expensive but direct
  3. Generic molecular AI methods (drug-discovery-oriented) — would need heavy adaptation to SME domain
  4. DeePTB-NEGF (2024) — accelerates transport simulation but does not address molecular generation or hypothesis-driven discovery

SME-Agent's innovation is not competing with an existing crowded field — it is creating a new field at the intersection of agentic AI and single-molecule electronics. This is both an opportunity (first-mover) and a risk (the approach is unproven in this domain).

1. Deep-Learning-Accelerated Quantum Transport (DeePTB-NEGF)

Core work (2024–2025)

Deep Learning Accelerated Quantum Transport Simulations in Nanoelectronics: From Break Junctions to Field-Effect Transistors
Jijie Zou, Zhanghao Zhouyin, Dongying Lin, Yike Huang, Linfeng Zhang, Shimin Hou, Qiangqiang Gu
arXiv:2411.08800v3 (Nov 2024, updated Jun 2025)
★★★★★ — The single most relevant AI-on-SME work from 2024+
DeePTB-NEGF — an integrated framework combining deep learning tight-binding Hamiltonian prediction with NEGF methodology. Key achievements:
• Predicts tight-binding Hamiltonians from geometry via deep neural networks
• 2-3 orders of magnitude acceleration over DFT+NEGF
• Demonstrated on break junction simulations with >10⁴ snapshots, matching experimental conductance histograms
• Scaled to 41nm CNT-FET (~8,000 atoms, 3×10⁴ orbitals) and 180nm CNT (~3×10⁴ atoms, 10⁵ orbitals)
• Systematic geometry variation studies confirm need for realistic experimental structures
transport simulation tight-binding NEGF 2024
Deep Learning-Based Quantum Transport Simulations in Two-Dimensional Materials
Jijie Zou, Zhanghao Zhouyin, Qiangqiang Gu, Shishir Kumar Pandey
arXiv:2512.11291v3 (Dec 2025, updated Jun 2026)
★★★★☆ — Extension of DeePTB to 2D materials; same methodology
Extends the DeePTB-NEGF framework to 2D material devices. Uses deep learning to predict tight-binding Hamiltonians for 2D material-based nanoelectronics, enabling efficient quantum transport simulations at experimental device scales.

Previous DeePTB-related work

Combining linear-scaling quantum transport and machine-learning molecular dynamics to study thermal and electronic transports in complex materials
Zheyong Fan, Yang Xiao, Yanzhou Wang, Penghua Ying, Shunda Chen, Haikuan Dong
arXiv:2310.15314 (2023)
ML-based neuroevolution potential combined with linear-scaling quantum transport (NEGF-DFT). Predicts thermal and electronic transport simultaneously. Relevant as precursor to the DeePTB approach, but focused on bulk/complex materials rather than molecular junctions.

Deep learning of deformation-dependent conductance in thin films: nanobubbles in graphene
Jack G. Nedell, Jonah Spector, Adel Abbout, Michael Vogl, Gregory A. Fiete
arXiv:2108.01057 (2021)
CNN approach predicting transport properties from deformation height maps. Focus on graphene nanobubbles rather than molecular junctions, but shares the idea of using deep learning to bypass expensive transport calculations.
CNNdeformation-conductance2021
Rapid Exploration of Topological Band Structures using Deep Learning
Vittorio Peano, Florian Sapper, Florian Marquardt
arXiv:1912.03296 (2019)
Deep learning for rapid exploration of topological band structures in periodic nanostructures. Different domain (topological photonics/phononics) but methodologically related to the idea of learning transport-relevant features from structure.
topological bands2019

Relationship to SME-Agent

DeePTB-NEGF is the closest existing AI-on-SME work. It accelerates the simulation step but does not address molecular generation, hypothesis formulation, or agentic discovery. SME-Agent's Quantum-Transport Agent could potentially use DeePTB-NEGF as one of its multi-fidelity simulation tools for rapid initial screening before full DFT+NEGF. However, DeePTB-NEGF requires training on DFT data for each system type — a non-trivial integration challenge.

2. ML for Break-Junction Data Analysis

2024+ works

Identifying Clean and Contaminated Atomic-Sized Gold Contacts under Ambient Conditions Using a Clustering Algorithm
Guillem Pellicer, Carlos Sabater
arXiv:2506.09608 (Jun 2025)
★★★☆☆ — ML for classifying break junction data quality
Uses clustering algorithms to identify clean vs contaminated gold contacts from break junction measurements under ambient conditions. Relevant for automated quality control in the experimental EC-STMBJ pipeline. Not directly about molecular junctions (focuses on bare gold contacts) but addresses a practical experimental challenge.
clusteringcontact quality2025
Improving single-molecule conductance measurements with change point detection from the econometrics toolbox
Joseph M. Hamill, William Bro-Jørgensen, Zoltán Balogh, Haixing Li, Susanne Leitherer, David Solomon, András Halbritter, Gemma Solomon
arXiv:2401.12769 (Jan 2024)
★★★★☆ — Applied ML method for SM BJ data analysis
Applies change point detection (from econometrics) to break junction conductance traces. Detects structural breaks in conductance vs displacement data corresponding to molecular junction formation, evolution, and rupture. Provides automated, statistically rigorous analysis pipeline. Directly relevant to the SME-Agent experimental module's data processing needs.
change point detection automated analysis 2024

Pre-2024 foundational works

Unsupervised Segmentation-Based Machine Learning as an Advanced Analysis Tool for Single Molecule Break Junction Data
Nathan D. Bamberger, Jeffrey A. Ivie, Keshaba N. Parida, Dominic V. McGrath, Oliver L. A. Monti
arXiv:2004.10731 (2020)
Segmentation-based ML for automated analysis of break junction conductance traces. Demonstrates robust feature extraction from large stochastic datasets without human bias. Important methodological reference for the SME-Agent experimental data pipeline.
segmentationunsupervised2020
Unsupervised feature recognition in single molecule break junction data
A. Magyarkuti, N. Balogh, Z. Balogh, L. Venkataraman, A. Halbritter
arXiv:2001.03006 (2020)
Unsupervised ML for recognizing features in break junction conductance traces. Enables automated classification of junction configurations without labeled training data. Complements the supervised and change-point approaches above.
feature recognitionunsupervised2020

Gap for SME-Agent

All existing ML-on-BJ-data work focuses on analyzing experimental traces rather than using experimental data to guide molecular design. There is no published work that closes the loop from BJ measurements back to generative model improvement. This is precisely what SME-Agent's active learning loop aims to achieve.

3. ML for Molecular Adsorption & Surface Chemistry

MAD-SURF: a machine learning interatomic potential for molecular adsorption on coinage metal surfaces
Manuel González Lastre, Joakim S. Jestilä, Rubén Pérez, Adam S. Foster
arXiv:2601.18852v2 (Jan 2026)
★★★☆☆ — MLIP for molecule-metal interfaces relevant to junction geometry
ML interatomic potential (MAD-SURF) for predicting molecular adsorption on coinage metal surfaces (Au, Ag, Cu). Central to surface chemistry and molecular electronics. Relevant for modeling the Au–molecule–Au junction geometry that is critical for accurate DFT+NEGF transport calculations. However, does not directly compute transport properties — focuses on adsorption geometries and energetics.
MLIPadsorptionAu surface2026
Paying attention to long-range electron correlation: a size-independent deep-learning approach to predicting molecules' electronic energies
Valerii Chuiko, Giovanni B. Da Rosa, Paul W. Ayers
arXiv:2504.03849 (2025)
★★☆☆☆ — ML for electronic structure, but general (not SME-specific)
Deep learning approach for predicting molecular electronic energies using one- and two-electron integrals. Size-independent, translationally/rotationally/ unitarily invariant descriptors. General quantum chemistry ML — not specific to molecular junctions or transport.
electronic structuredeep learning2025

Gap for SME-Agent

These MLIPs address the geometry prediction problem but not the transport prediction or molecular design problems. SME-Agent's Quantum-Transport Agent could potentially leverage MAD-SURF for more accurate junction geometry sampling before transport calculations.

Critical observation

The fact that SMEPre remains unpublished (it exists only in the legacy manuscript draft) reinforces the point: there is essentially no peer-reviewed, published AI system for single-molecule electronics property prediction or molecular design. The field is wide open.

6. Critical Gap Analysis & SME-Agent Positioning

What exists (2024–2026)

  1. DeePTB-NEGF (Zou et al., 2024) — ML-accelerated DFT+NEGF simulation. Accelerates transport computation but does not generate molecules, formulate hypotheses, or guide discovery.
  2. Change-point detection for BJ data (Hamill et al., 2024) — automated analysis of experimental conductance traces. Improves data processing but does not close the design loop.
  3. Clustering for contact quality (Pellicer & Sabater, 2025) — experimental quality control for break junctions.
  4. MAD-SURF (Lastre et al., 2026) — MLIP for molecule-metal adsorption geometries. Improves junction modeling but does not predict transport or design molecules.

What does NOT exist (gap analysis)

  1. No generative AI for SME molecules. No published work applies graph diffusion, VAEs, Transformers, or any generative model to single-molecule electronics molecular design. SME-Agent's Generative Design Agent fills this gap.
  2. No ML predictor for quantum-transport properties. The only existing predictor (SMEPre) is unpublished and lacks UQ. SME-Agent's predictor ensemble with uncertainty quantification fills this gap.
  3. No hypothesis-driven molecular discovery for SME. No system formulates testable quantum-transport hypotheses and designs molecules to test them. SME-Agent's Hypothesis Agent fills this gap.
  4. No multi-agent system for SME. No existing framework combines literature mining, generation, validation, synthesis assessment, and critique for molecular electronics. SME-Agent's six-agent architecture fills this gap.
  5. No active learning loop for SME. No published work closes the DFT+NEGF → retrain → regenerate loop for molecular junction discovery. SME-Agent's active learning workflow fills this gap.
  6. No benchmark comparing AI vs expert rules vs random for SME. No matched-budget comparison of molecular design strategies for quantum-transport properties. SME-Agent's benchmark plan fills this gap.
  7. No systematic experimental validation of AI-designed SME molecules with controls. The legacy 3-molecule validation lacks negatives, expert controls, and isomer controls. SME-Agent's 8-15 molecule validation plan fills this gap.

SME-Agent manuscript positioning statement

Based on this survey, the strongest claim SME-Agent can make (once validated) is:

"We present the first AI-driven, multi-agent molecular discovery system for single-molecule electronics. The system integrates hypothesis generation, quantum-interference-conditioned molecular design, uncertainty-aware transport prediction, DFT+NEGF validation, retrosynthetic assessment, and prospective experimental testing — a combination that does not exist in any prior work."

Key risks for the novelty claim

  1. The emptiness of the field is a double-edged sword: It means SME-Agent is a first mover, but also that the approach is unvalidated in this domain. Every component (graph diffusion for SME, QI-conditioned generation, hypothesis-driven design) is unproven for SME molecules.
  2. Data scarcity is acute: ~171 Tier A molecules is an extremely small dataset for training both generative and predictive models. Transfer learning from larger chemical spaces will be essential but may not transfer well to the specialized SME molecular subspace.
  3. DFT+NEGF cost vs depth: The DeePTB-NEGF work shows that ML-accelerated transport is feasible, but integrating it requires substantial software engineering. Without it, the DFT bottleneck (50-150 molecules/round) will limit the active learning loop's statistical power.
  4. Comparison baseline challenge: Since no other AI system exists for SME, the baseline comparison is against expert-designed molecules (from Lambert group rules) and random enumeration. A strong result would beat expert rules — but expert rules are themselves powerful and well-tested.
  5. Reviewer expectation management: Reviewers unfamiliar with agentic AI may see the system as "just a pipeline" rather than a true agentic discovery system. The manuscript must make the agentic contribution explicit.

Bottom line

SME-Agent is not entering a crowded field — it is creating one. The survey shows that between 2024 and 2026, there is exactly one paper (DeePTB-NEGF) that applies deep learning to molecular junction transport, and it addresses only the simulation acceleration problem. The remaining 2024+ AI-on-SME work is limited to data analysis (change point detection) and experimental quality control (clustering). No generative model, no predictor with UQ, no agentic system, no active learning loop, and no hypothesis-driven discovery exists for single-molecule electronics. If SME-Agent delivers on its architecture, it will be genuinely novel in both methods and domain.

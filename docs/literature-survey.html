1. Single-Molecule Electronics & Quantum Interference

Domain overview

Single-molecule electronics studies charge transport through individual molecules sandwiched between electrodes. Quantum interference (QI) — both constructive (CQI) and destructive (DQI) — dramatically modulates conductance and is the central physical mechanism the SME-Agent project seeks to harness. Key themes include the magic ratio rule (MRR), connectivity-driven QI, heteroatom substitution effects, gate-controlled transport, and the transition from fundamental physics to functional devices.

Foundation and theory

Basic concepts in single-molecule electronics
C. J. Lambert
arXiv:1601.00224 (2016) — Tutorial
Outlines the basic theoretical concepts for phase-coherent electron transport through single molecules. The transmission coefficient T(E) is the central quantity, yielding conductance, thermopower, and current–voltage characteristics. Establishes the Landauer–Büttiker formalism as the foundation for molecular junction transport.

Provides chemical design principles for exploiting QI in molecular-scale devices. The magic ratio rule (MRR) connects molecular connectivity to conductance ratios, giving chemists a simple predictive framework. Covers PAHs, graphene-like molecules, and heteroatom-substituted systems.

Gap identified for SME-Agent

While the MRR and connectivity rules are well established for simple aromatics, no systematic AI-driven exploration of novel QI motifs beyond known expert rules exists. The field has strong heuristic design rules for common cases (benzene, naphthalene, OPE, PAHs) but lacks automated tools for discovering counterintuitive or non-classical QI behavior. The SME-Agent hypothesis agent is designed to fill exactly this gap.

2. AI for Molecular Discovery & Generative Design

Domain overview

AI-driven molecular generation has matured rapidly, moving from SMILES-based LSTMs (2017–2019) through VAEs and GANs to diffusion models and Transformer-based approaches. Reinforcement learning (REINVENT-style) remains a strong practical baseline. The key challenge for SME is conditioning generation on quantum-transport properties (DQI, Seebeck, gating) rather than drug-discovery properties (logP, QED, docking score), which have been the main focus of existing work.

Gap identified for SME-Agent

Almost no generative models condition on quantum-transport properties. SME-Agent's Generative Design Agent would be among the first to condition generation on DQI features, Seebeck coefficient, regulation range, and anchor-group compatibility — a fundamentally different chemical space.

3. Graph Diffusion & Graph Transformers for Molecular Generation

Domain overview

Graph diffusion models have become the state of the art for molecular generation, offering better coverage of chemical space and more natural handling of molecular graphs compared to SMILES-based approaches. Conditional graph diffusion (DiGress, GDSS, etc.) enables property-guided generation. Graph Transformers add global attention to GNN inductive biases for property prediction. The SME-Agent project targets a conditional graph diffusion generator as its primary model.

Gap identified for SME-Agent

Existing graph diffusion models are trained on drug-like chemical spaces (QM9, ZINC, ChEMBL). 4. Agentic AI for Scientific Discovery

Domain overview

Agentic AI — systems that reason, plan, use tools, and pursue goals with some autonomy — has rapidly emerged as a paradigm for scientific discovery. Multiple frameworks and surveys were published in 2024–2026, covering domains from drug discovery to materials science to astronomy. The SME-Agent project sits squarely in this emerging paradigm, applying multi-agent principles to the specialized domain of single-molecule electronics.

5. Uncertainty-Quantified Molecular Property Prediction

Domain overview

Uncertainty quantification (UQ) in molecular property prediction is critical for reliable decision-making in active learning and experimental design. Techniques include Bayesian neural networks, ensembles, MC dropout, and evidential regression. For SME-Agent, UQ is essential for distinguishing genuine molecular signals from predictor noise, especially given the small dataset.

6. Retrosynthesis, Synthetic Accessibility & Active Learning

Domain overview

Synthetic accessibility scoring (SAscore, RAscore) and retrosynthesis planning (AiZynthFinder, CASP) are essential filters between molecule generation and experimental validation. Active learning (AL) connects computational screening to experimental feedback. For SME-Agent, these ensure that beautiful but unsynthesizable molecules do not dominate the discovery pipeline.

Gap identified for SME-Agent

Existing retrosynthesis and synthetic accessibility tools are not optimized for single-molecule electronics requirements. Specifically, they do not account for anchor-group compatibility, electrochemical stability under STM junction conditions, or synthesis of molecules designed for specific QI behavior. SME-Agent's Synthesis Agent must adapt these general-purpose tools to the unique constraints of molecular junction synthesis.

Innovation dimensions

1. Domain-specific agentic architecture for molecular electronics
No existing agentic framework targets single-molecule electronics. SME-Agent's six-agent architecture (Literature, Hypothesis, Generative Design, Quantum-Transport, Synthesis, Critic) is designed specifically for the SME discovery workflow.
2. Hypothesis-driven generation, not black-box optimization
Unlike standard generative models that maximize a scalar property score, SME-Agent generates molecules to test explicit quantum-transport hypotheses. This shifts the evaluation from "property value" to "hypothesis confirmation value" — a fundamentally different discovery paradigm.
3. Quantum-transport conditioning for molecular generation
No existing generative model conditions on DQI labels, transmission spectra, Seebeck coefficients, or regulation range. This requires new training data, new conditioning architectures, and new evaluation metrics.
4. Uncertainty-aware prediction in a sparse, high-value domain
The SME-Agent predictor ensemble with explicit uncertainty quantification is designed for a domain where DFT calculations are expensive ($50-150 molecules per round) and experimental validation is even more costly. Every decision must account for confidence.
5. Matched-budget comparison with baselines
The benchmark plan includes 6 generators (agentic, graph diffusion, REINVENT4, LSTM, expert rules, random) compared under matched DFT+NEGF budgets — a rigor standard that most molecular discovery papers do not meet.
6. Built-in critique and reviewer protection
The Critic Agent explicitly protects against overclaiming, train/test leakage, cherry-picking, and known-QI-rule rediscovery. This is a novel architectural component not found in any existing molecular discovery system.

SME-Agent's unique claims-in-waiting

Claims that can be made post-validation

  1. First agentic discovery framework for single-molecule electronics — no existing system combines literature mining, hypothesis generation, QI-conditioned design, DFT+NEGF validation, retrosynthesis filtering, and critic review.
  2. First conditional graph diffusion model for quantum-transport properties — no existing generative model conditions on DQI labels, transmission features, or Seebeck coefficients.
  3. Systematic comparison of 6 generation strategies under matched DFT budgets — most papers compare only 2-3 methods; SME-Agent benchmarks agentic, graph diffusion, REINVENT4, LSTM, expert rules, and random baselines.
  4. Prospective experimental validation with positive, negative, and control molecules — the legacy validation of 3 molecules is expanded to 8-15 molecules with proper controls.
  5. Demonstration that hypothesis-guided generation outperforms property-maximizing generation — the core scientific claim distinguishing SME-Agent from conventional closed-loop optimization.

Key risks and open questions

  1. Small dataset problem: The SME dataset (~171 Tier A molecules) is extremely small by ML standards. Transfer learning, pre-training, or data augmentation strategies are likely needed.
  2. DFT-NEGF bottleneck: Even with surrogate filtering, DFT+NEGF validation for 50-150 molecules per round is computationally expensive. Need to ensure the quantum-transport agent is efficient.
  3. Hypothesis agent quality: LLM-generated quantum-transport hypotheses may be plausible-sounding but physically wrong. The scoring function (novelty, plausibility, testability) must be robust.
  4. Synthetic feasibility for novel scaffolds: The most interesting AI-discovered molecules may be the hardest to synthesize. The Synthesis Agent must balance novelty with practicality.
  5. Experimental bandwidth: EC-STMBJ characterization of 8-15 molecules requires significant experimental resources. The validation plan must be realistic about throughput.
  6. Risk of rediscovery: The biggest scientific risk is that the "AI-discovered" molecules are actually just re-expressions of known magic-ratio rule molecules. The Critic Agent's novelty analysis is essential.
